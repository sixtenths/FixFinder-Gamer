# LCD evidence and bring-up

## Reference and board observations

- The [Wayton MFC028D-BQ sheet](Wayton-MFC028D-BQ.pdf) describes a 240 x 320 normally white panel with MCU or four-wire SPI capability. Its drawing numbers 40 flex contacts. The table gives a 2.4-3.3 V module supply and identifies data pins 3-20, SDA 21, read/enable 26, write/read-write 28, D/C or serial clock 29, reset 30, IM0-IM3 at 31-34, INSEL 35, ground 36 and LED anode/cathode at 39/40. Pin 27 repeats the HSYNC label. The table omits an explicit chip-select entry, controller ID, mode strap truth table and LED current rating.
- [Photo 261](photos/261.jpg) confirms the installed module model `MFC028D-BQ` and carries the additional marking `22091`. [Photo 276](photos/276.jpg) shows the populated LCD connector with 40 visible solder contacts. PCB labels 1 and 40 sit near C5/C7 and Q1/L1, respectively. [Photo 262](photos/262.jpg) shows the separate unpopulated U5 footprint labeled 1/24 and 25/48.
- Photo 263 shows the panel flex marked CN2, with 1 and 48 printed at opposite ends. The revision string appears to read `FPC-C028D2-4RH`. The first character after `FPC-` could be G. The Wayton drawing and PCB connector agree on contact count. The electrical correspondence needs a continuity check because the installed flex carries different markings.
- A group of traces fans out from the LCD connector in photos 264 and 276. A parallel interface is a hypothesis worth checking. Q1 has G/D/S labels and sits next to the pin-40 end. It is a candidate backlight switch. FB1, L1 and the flex K/A pads give additional trace points. The gate GPIO, rail voltage and polarity are open.
- [Photo 277](photos/277.jpg) shows both numbered ends of the populated LCD connector and the adjacent side of MCU U3 in one frame. [Photo 280](photos/280.jpg) gives a second angle on the fan-out near TP18–TP21 and the R13/R17 and C6 footprints. The group of closely spaced tracks supports investigating a parallel bus. The images leave gaps between individual connector contacts and MCU legs in this area, so they do not establish the data order, chip select, write strobe or reset net.
- The rear silkscreen identifies the board as `CB204001301` and `PCB_2L_5034_REVC_TYPEC_20200319`. [Photo 285](photos/285.jpg) shows the unpopulated U5 footprint with two rows of 24 pads. The attached flex sits below the board edge and plugs into the separate front connector. A registration check using the J5 and P2 through-holes in [photo 278](photos/278.jpg) and [photo 282](photos/282.jpg) gave a coarse overlay. Affine and perspective fits differed by roughly 10–16 pixels at the LCD connector in the rear view. Matching nearby circular features reduced the error at the original anchors, but the resulting projection missed independent board edge landmarks. The photos still leave individual contact and via assignments unresolved.
- [Photo 286](photos/286.jpg) shows all eleven keypad contacts, S1 through S11. Their physical locations are visible. The signal mapping to MCU GPIOs remains open.
- A second registration check compared the visible solder row in photo 264 against both candidate rear P2 rows. Either row fits the narrow set of connector landmarks within a pixel. Adding landmarks where the flex crosses the board edge exposed errors of several pixels or more and shifted the projected write and reset contacts. That rules out using the connector fit alone as evidence for a GPIO assignment.
- A wider registration used the two mounting holes and J5 in photos 278 and 283, then transferred the result to photo 282 using shared rear landmarks. The fit based on J5 and the mounting holes still misplaced the ribbon at the board edge. Adding the ribbon and both candidate P2 positions left maximum landmark errors of approximately 20–28 pixels in photo 282, where adjacent projected LCD contacts are about five pixels apart. The wider views do not resolve the reset and write paths at contact-level accuracy.
- During a USB power cycle on V8, the user saw the backlight come on with the screen still black. Prioritize the LCD controller's logic supply, reset and data interface for the first pixel test. The observation leaves the backlight circuit topology open.
- The role of U6 near the connector is unknown. NRD_E, NWR_RNW, DNC_SCL and VPP_OTP appear together in both the HX8347-D(T) and HX8347-G(T) datasheets. The panel label confirms the Wayton module model and supplies no controller marking. Once the electrical interface and mode are verified, the documented D(T) check is page 0 register `0x00`, which should return `0x47` in register-content mode. The G(T) preliminary sheet lists `0x75` in prose, while its ID register figure depicts `0x47`. A single `0x47` therefore warrants further corroboration before selecting a controller-specific power sequence. Use the measured data lanes, bus turnaround and read timing. Treat connector pin 27 as unknown until traced. [HX8347-D(T) datasheet](https://www.displayfuture.com/engineering/specs/controller/HX8347-D.pdf), [HX8347-G(T) preliminary datasheet](https://cdn-shop.adafruit.com/datasheets/HX8347-G_DS_T_preliminary_v01_100203.pdf)
- The locally installed Innova updater lists variants `0x064A` and `0x064B`. Its versioned temporary cache had no package for either model. The updater's metadata endpoint returned no firmware for both model-only queries, which supplied neither a device GUID nor a customer ID. This route yielded no stock LCD initialization to inspect.
- Offline extraction of the Innova 1.3.9 installer yielded 126 files containing the Windows application, libraries, symbols and resources. The extracted files contained neither an identifiable device firmware payload nor either vector signature from the saved Cortex-M fragment. The updater's temporary cache held two device records. Its official update check accepted the cached device and customer IDs with the app's zero-padded version format and returned code `7002` with no firmware or download URL for either 5034VSM USB variant.
- The historical `FixFinder-Decompiled.zip` contains decompiled Windows updater code. Its firmware utilities describe how to recognize downloaded main firmware and bootloader packages. The archive itself has no device firmware payload. The [Wayton product page](https://www.wayton.com.tw/goods1-60.html) lists both MCU and four-wire SPI capability and gives no controller model.
- The local `FixFinder-Storage-Backup` includes eight identical 20,829-byte `VehData/*/OBD2_LDRecord.bin` files containing Cortex-M code. The first vector table leads to a boot stub that checks for an application and jumps to its reset handler. A second vector table appears at file offset `0x4000`. Code near file offset `0x50d2` passes a white RGB565 value and bounds `(0, 0)` through `(319, 239)` to a probable fill routine. The called fill and preceding setup routines resolve beyond the saved fragment when disassembled at a common base. This fragment supplies evidence for landscape rendering but does not establish the GPIO wiring or controller.
- In the fragment, offsets `0x0800` through `0x3fff` contain erased bytes and the file ends at offset `0x515c`. The display setup and fill calls target offsets `0x5adc` and `0x5b24` under a contiguous decode. The downloaded Innova updater installer does not contain either of the fragment's raw vector signatures. The local updater settings folder yielded only its configuration file.
- The second scanner enumerated as stock `1720:064A` and reported firmware `V24.00.04` and bootloader `V23.00.03`. Windows reports a 3,088-byte HID feature buffer. The system-info and GUID requests returned distinct responses, confirming the USB command channel. The updater's `ReadMemory` packet returned the previous system-info response at both absolute and relative flash addresses, including the LCD setup offset `0x5adc`. The tested `ReadFlash` report variants also returned that response. Neither factory vector signature appeared in the captures. The service storage interface exposed a drive letter but no readable medium during the Innova storage enable check. The storage disable command was sent after the check. This USB session yielded no new factory code.

## First continuity measurements

After the J5 leads were reseated on the stock scanner, it enumerated normally over USB. Probe-rs could not read the core ID. OpenOCD's native Pi GPIO driver read Arm SWD debug port ID `0x2ba01477` repeatedly at 2 and 20 kHz, but could not find the memory access port. Neither tool reached the protection or LCD configuration registers. The protection level is unknown. The checks used no flash or option-byte writes.

Moving the same leads back to scanner 1 provided a comparison with the previously working target. Windows recognized its expected V8 USB instance. Probe-rs could not read the core ID at 50 kHz. OpenOCD failed to establish debug access at 2 and 20 kHz. Both scanners therefore had debug communication failures with the current Pi and lead setup, so the scanner 2 result alone cannot establish a protection issue. The Pi SWD pins were returned to inputs after the check.

After checking the Pi header and replacing the jumpers, scanner 1 passed the comparison. Probe-rs at 50 kHz read the expected core ID, board UID and V8 boot vectors. OpenOCD at 20 kHz read the same SWD port ID observed on scanner 2 and successfully examined scanner 1's Cortex-M4 core.

With the replacement jumpers on scanner 2, OpenOCD at 2 kHz found MEM-AP 0 (`0x24770011`) and read CPUID `0x410fc241`. Cortex-M polling reported a lockup and reexamined the core. Follow-up reads used the `mem_ap` target with polling disabled. `FMC_OBCTL0` read `0x2fff00ed` twice, which gives SPC `0x00` (low protection). The EXMC clock was enabled and the TLI clock was disabled. Windows still enumerated the stock USB device after the captures. The Pi SWD pins were returned to inputs.

The stock configuration enabled 8-bit, nonmultiplexed, asynchronous SRAM/NOR region 2 with writes enabled (`SNCTL2=0x00001081`, `SNTCFG2=0x00000615`, `SNWTCFG2=0x00000000`; extended timing disabled). Regions 0 and 1 were disabled. The GPIO mux readback gives AF12 on PD14, PD15, PD0, PD1 and PE7–PE10 for EXMC D0–D7, PD4 for NOE, PD5 for NWE, PG10 for NE2, and PF0 for A0. PD2 was also configured AF12; the GD32F427 pin table does not assign it an EXMC function. The stock settings point to an 8-bit LCD transport at the region 2 window (`0x68000000`) with A0 as the candidate command/data select. The board-to-flex routing, command/data polarity, reset net, and controller initialization still need corroboration before a green test. [GigaDevice pin table](https://www.gd32mcu.com/data/documents/datasheet/GD32F427xx_Datasheet_Rev1.5.pdf)

An SRAM-only probe on scanner 1 used this EXMC mapping, byte writes at `0x68000000` for commands, and byte reads and writes at `0x68000001` for data. With the reset GPIO untouched, commands `0x04` and `0xda` both returned `00 e3 00 00`. Command `0x09` returned `00 00 61 00` and `0x00` returned zeros. The read results matched with no pulls, weak pull-up and weak pull-down. This corroborates the command/data polarity and the DCS protocol. The exact controller and reset GPIO remain unknown.

The RAM green test finished without a CPU fault. Panel power status `0x0a` changed from `0x08` to `0x9c` and pixel format `0x0c` from `0x06` to `0x05` after the DCS wake, format and display-on commands. The user saw a fully unlit screen. `0x2e` returned dummy `0x00` then repeated `0xfc` bytes after the full RGB565 green stream and after small RGB565 and RGB666 color patterns. Holding the known chip-select pin active for a short pixel transfer gave the same readback. This leaves pixel storage unverified. The stock GPIO snapshot configured PF7 as AF3 and enabled TIMER10's clock, making its channel 0 a candidate for backlight PWM. Read that timer on the factory board before applying any backlight settings.

After a cold USB reconnect, the user observed a faint backlight under V8. A second RAM run enabled normal display mode before streaming the pixels, matched the factory drive strength on NOE and NWE, and synchronized each data byte before issuing the next. It reached the completion marker with clear Cortex-M fault registers. The user confirmed that the physical panel showed dark green and appeared dimly backlit. The panel returned `0x9c` for power, `0x05` for pixel format, and `00 fc fc fc` for the first pixel read. The visible fill confirms that the panel displayed the test color. The GRAM readback sequence still needs investigation, and the probe does not drive the backlight.

With scanner 2 visibly lit, read-only captures returned TIMER10 `CTL0=0x81`, `CHCTL0=0x60`, `CHCTL2=0x01`, `PSC=0`, `CAR=0xffff`, and `CH0CV=0x9999`. The intermittent debug link required separate captures. Together with the factory PF7 AF3 setting, these values describe an enabled, active-high PWM output at about 60% duty. The optional `FIXFINDER_PROBE_BACKLIGHT` RAM build uses that configuration on scanner 1 after confirming its panel ID. The exact PWM frequency depends on the running MCU clock.

The backlight-enabled RAM build ran on scanner 1 after confirming its UID and V8 vectors. It reached the green-test completion marker with clear Cortex-M fault registers. Timer readback confirmed channel 0 enabled, a running counter, prescaler zero, reload `0xffff` and compare `0x9999`. The user confirmed that the screen was clearly brighter green than the previous test. This verifies PF7/TIMER10 as the backlight control on scanner 1. The diagnostic is volatile and a USB power cycle returns the scanner to V8.

A public schematic search for `CB204001301`, `PCB_2L_5034_REVC_TYPEC_20200319`, `5034VSM` and model `1009352` found no matching board document. [Innova's manual index](https://innova-electronics.helpscoutdocs.com/article/2693-innova-product-manuals) has no 5034 entry. The public FCC filings indexed for [Innova Electronics Inc](https://fcc.report/company/innova-electronics-inc) and [Innova Electronics, Corp.](https://fcc.report/FRN/0029654365) list other products. The Wayton sheet provides panel-side signals only. A schematic or netlist for the exact Rev C board, or complete stock LCD initialization code, would let us continue without a meter.

Use only the experimental board. Disconnect USB and the Pi leads before measuring resistance. Short the probes together first to establish the lead resistance.

| Point A | Point B | What a near-zero result establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LCD connector contact 40, the bottommost solder leg by the 40 silkscreen in photo 276 | Q1 drain, the single large pad next to the D marking | Same PCB net. This supports the switched LED return hypothesis if the flex cathode is on that contact. |
| LCD connector contact 36, the fifth solder leg up from the pin-40 end in photo 276 | J5 hole 6, the GND reference from the SWD mapping | Same ground net and one anchor consistent with the Wayton table. |

Record actual ohms or OL. Check continuity to the flex K and A pads with the flex seated before assigning backlight nets. Trace the Q1 source to its reference and the gate through any resistor to a named MCU pad. Confirm the logic supply and LED circuit separately.

After establishing orientation and connector correspondence, test LCD contact 28 against U3 PD5 (package leg 119, EXMC NWE) and LCD contact 26 against PD4 (leg 118, EXMC NOE). If they match, trace PD7 (leg 123, NE0) and check the connector fan-out against EXMC data pins. Useful anchors are PD14 (leg 85, D0), PE7 (58, D4), PE11 (64, D8) and PD10 (79, D15). Record the actual connector contact for each because MCU D0 and panel DB0 may differ. If the strobes fail to match, trace their board nets before choosing GPIO or SPI. ## V9 first hardware check

Confirm interface straps, reset, command/data, chip select, bus direction, supply and backlight circuit before driving outputs. If the controller is still unknown after wiring is confirmed, an ID transaction requires approval as a hardware-control experiment. Start the diagnostic from the V8 USBHS code path. Initialize the confirmed controller, fill a single color without allocating a framebuffer, then cycle red, green, blue, white and black. Verify HID enumeration and reset behavior before adding text or inputs.

The candidate HX8347 register layer in `firmware/lcd/hx8347.c` is ready for a verified board transport. It checks two page-zero ID reads for consistency, programs the inclusive window registers `0x02` through `0x09`, and streams RGB565 to GRAM `0x22`. The board transport is responsible for reset, power sequencing, lane mapping, chip select and bus turnaround. The register layer stays separate from the current firmware build while those details are open.

The candidate adapter in `firmware/lcd/doom_hx8347.c` sends the game's landscape output through the same GRAM window. It reuses the existing scanline and closes the transfer after the last row or the first failed write. The game build with `-BoardSource` uses the initialized transport and the board's debounced action callback. The headless build still consumes rows in software.

The V9 color routine in `firmware/lcd/v9_color_test.c` takes a verified, initialized HX8347 transport and a delay callback. It requires the expected raw ID to corroborate the identified controller before writing a window. It displays red, green, blue, white and black for one second each and leaves black on screen. The `v9_lcd_progress` symbol exposes the phase, observed ID and current or last attempted RGB565 color for SWD inspection.

Pass `-SolidGreen` to `scripts/Build-V9.ps1` for a power-cycle observation. This variant fills the screen once and leaves it green after a successful controller check.

`scripts/Build-V9.ps1` first verifies a byte-identical V8 build, then reuses its USB and startup objects. It requires `-BoardSource` with an implementation of `v9_lcd_board_init` from `firmware/lcd/v9_board.h`. That implementation must supply measured pin mappings and a verified controller power and setup sequence. The V9 entry calls it after USB interrupt setup and uses the V8 Timer2 delay. It records a board-init failure and returns to the USB application's idle loop if initialization fails. The linker writes a separate ELF, BIN and map under `build/`. A mock transport linked and exercised both entry paths with USB calls stubbed in emulation. The physical board transport remains pending.

V9 is limited at link time to the 16 KiB sector at `0x08000000`. The build produces complete `FixFinder-LCD-V9-sector0.bin` and `FixFinder-USB-V8-sector0.bin` images and prints their SHA-256 hashes. The V8 sector image extends the verified V8 binary with erased bytes. Before a temporary V9 test, identify the experimental board by UID and compare a fresh read of the entire first sector with the generated V8 sector image. A mismatch requires investigation before writing. After the approved test, restore the verified sector and compare the full sector again. [probe-rs 0.32.0 target layout](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/probe-rs/probe-rs/v0.32.0/probe-rs/targets/GD32F4xx_Series.yaml#L2541-L2573)

Run `python scripts/Check-LcdProtocol.py` with the same Unicorn and pyelftools dependencies as the DOOM smoke test. The mock bus verifies portrait and landscape bounds, all 76,800 pixels in both a color fill and a scaled indexed game frame, the V9 color sequence, and closure of the pixel stream on a transfer failure. It also checks that the routine stops before writing a window if the ID is unreadable or mismatched.
